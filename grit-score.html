The Grit Score //
Scoring Protocol

Five metrics. One number. Zero marketing.

Scale 0 – 10
Metrics 5 Weighted
Method Field-Tested
Bias Zero

Verdict //

The Grit Score answers one question: will this gear survive the abuse you'll put it through?

Star ratings lie. Brand affinity lies. Lab specs lie. The Grit Score is built from five weighted metrics, scored only after field-testing across washboard, rain, and self-supported abuse. Every gear review on this site carries one. Every score links back here. No marketing exceptions.

01 // Durability (30%)

How long until it breaks under abuse. Tested across minimum 500 miles of mixed terrain including washboard, chunky gravel, and sustained loaded riding. Failure modes — torn seams, crushed shells, snapped buckles, cracked frames — get logged and weighted.

10/10: Will outlast you. 7/10: Race-season durable. 5/10: One-season piece. Below 5: Field-failure during test.

02 // Aero-Efficiency / Compliance (20%)

For frames and bags: measurable watts saved or vibration dampened. For tires: rolling resistance and impedance under realistic loadout. For wheels: rotational mass plus crosswind behavior. Lab data is referenced. Field data overrides.

10/10: Measurable advantage at race pace. 7/10: Perceived advantage on long days. 5/10: Neutral. Below 5: Active penalty.

03 // Repairability (20%)

Can you fix it in the field with a multi-tool, duct tape, and zip ties? Can a local shop fix it with standard parts? Or does it require a proprietary part from the manufacturer with a six-week lead time? Repairability is survival currency on a self-supported route.

10/10: Field-repairable with basic tools. 7/10: Shop-repairable with standard parts. 5/10: Manufacturer repair only. Below 5: Single-failure trip-ender.

04 // Weather Resistance (15%)

Performance after 12 hours of sustained rain. Performance after a freezing overnight. Performance covered in mud, hosed clean, and re-deployed. For electronics: IP rating verified, not just claimed. Marketing specs are starting points — field results are scores.

10/10: IPX7 verified, no thermal degradation. 7/10: Survives shower-grade rain. 5/10: Needs supplementary protection (dry sack, rain cover). Below 5: Fails in any sustained weather.

05 // Weight-to-Function Ratio (15%)

Lightweight gear that doesn't do the job is not lightweight — it's a liability you carried for nothing. Score = measured grams divided by measured field performance. Heavy gear with high function can still score well. Light gear that compromises function scores below 5.

10/10: Best-in-class weight, no functional compromise. 7/10: Acceptable weight penalty for the function. 5/10: Heavy but earns its weight. Below 5: Weight-weenie trash that fails under load.

Field Intel // How Scores Are Calculated

Each metric is scored 0–10 after field testing. The composite is the weighted sum:

Grit Score = (Durability × 0.30) + (Aero × 0.20) + (Repairability × 0.20) + (Weather × 0.15) + (Weight × 0.15)

Composite is rounded to one decimal. Anything above 9.0 is mandatory equipment. 8.0–8.9 is field-proven. 7.0–7.9 is conditional — solid in the right context, weak in the wrong one. Below 7.0 means we'd warn a friend.

Critical Warning //

A 9.5 Grit Score is not a recommendation to buy. Match the gear to your loadout, your route, and your tolerance for risk. A 9.5 ultra-racing bag is the wrong tool for a trans-continental expedition. The score tells you the gear works. You still have to pick the right one for the protocol.

Bottom Line //

Numbers are accountability. If we score it 9.5 and it fails you in the field, that's on us — and the score gets revised. The Grit Score is not a snapshot. It's a contract.
